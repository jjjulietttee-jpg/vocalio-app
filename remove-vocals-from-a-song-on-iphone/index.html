How to Remove Vocals From a Song on iPhone

Updated October 2026 · By the Acapo developer

Use an AI vocal remover app. Add the song, let the AI split it into voice and band, then mute the voice (in Acapo, the Karaoke preset). What is left is the karaoke track. This guide uses Acapo, the app we make, and compares it honestly with other options.

Quick answer. On iPhone, add a song you own to a vocal remover such as Acapo, wait about a minute while the AI separates it, then tap the Karaoke preset in the Mix tab to mute the voice. You hear the instrumental and can save it as a file. Mute the band instead and you have an acapella. Results are good on most songs but never perfect.

Step by step

  1. Add a songTap Add a song and pick the source: your music library, the Files app, a video (Acapo takes its soundtrack) or a microphone recording. Acapo asks once for your consent to send the audio to its cloud separation service.
  2. Wait for the splitAcapo separates the song in the cloud into two tracks, the voice and the band (instrumental). It usually takes under a minute, depending on song length and connection.
  3. Mute the voiceOpen the song and, in the Mix tab, tap the Karaoke preset (voice off, band full). You now hear only the band.
  4. Fit it to your voice (optional)In the Key tab, tap Fit my voice to move the whole song up or down without changing its speed. Range is up to 6 semitones either way.
  5. Save the track (Pro)Use the export menu to save the instrumental, or the vocals, as its own file.

When another app may suit you better

If you want separate drums, bass, guitar and piano, or an app that also runs on Android and the web, Moises is the bigger, more established choice. If you want to split a file on a computer or a very long file, LALAL.AI's web service has higher limits. Acapo is for singers who want two clean tracks and then tools to actually sing the song: key to your voice, loop, tempo and recording.

Frequently asked questions

Can you remove vocals from a song on iPhone for free?

You can download Acapo for free and try vocal removal on the built-in demo songs. Separating your own songs, and saving the result as files, needs Acapo Pro: weekly with a 3-day free trial, yearly or monthly, with the exact price shown in the app.

How do I make a karaoke track from any song?

Add the song to Acapo, wait for the split, then tap the Karaoke preset in the Mix tab. The band keeps playing as a karaoke track and you can move it into your key with Fit my voice.

How do I get an acapella of a song?

After the split, tap the Acapella preset in the Mix tab. Save the vocals as its own file from the export menu.

How long does it take?

Usually under a minute for a normal song. It depends on length and your connection, because the split runs in the cloud.

Is the result perfect?

No AI separation is. On dense mixes you may hear faint traces of the voice in the band track or the other way round. Clean studio recordings separate best.

Which sources can I use?

Songs in your music library that are stored as files, files from the Files app, the soundtrack of a video from your camera roll, or a recording you make with the microphone.

Can I split drums, bass and guitar?

No. Acapo splits a song into two tracks: voice and band. If you need separate drums, bass, guitar or piano, use an app built for multi-stem separation such as Moises.

Does Acapo work offline?

Not for separating a new song: that step runs on a cloud AI model and needs an internet connection. Key and tempo analysis happens on your iPhone, and songs that are already separated can be played, changed in key, looped and sung over without a connection.

Can I use Apple Music or Spotify songs?

No. Acapo only works with audio files you already have: your music library files, the Files app, the soundtrack of a video or a recording. It does not search, stream or download music, and streamed or copy-protected Apple Music songs cannot be read by any app.

Is it legal to remove vocals from songs I do not own?

For private practice and personal karaoke this is generally fine, but publishing or selling the result needs permission from the rights holders. Use music you have the right to use.
